22. Februarius

Dear Kohler,

You asked in your last letter whether my tower has made any difference to the town. I confess I did not expect to have much to report, but the evidence is becoming difficult to ignore.

The road between my tower and the village has been repaired. Every stone set flush. I counted zero uneven joints over the full distance, which I believe is unprecedented. The physician tells me he has not treated a fever since before winter. Brauer's boy, who broke his arm falling from the mill last autumn, healed in three weeks. The butcher's scales, which have been dishonest since before I arrived, now read true. I checked them myself.

The Bürgermeister stopped me in the square to shake my hand. He has never done this. He said the tower's ticking is a comfort to the whole town. That people sleep better. That there have been no disputes before him in six weeks. He seemed genuinely pleased and I admit I was gratified to hear it.

You will say I am making too much of a clock, and you may be right. But I passed the woodcarver Renner's shop on the way home and stopped to watch him work. He was making chairs. Four of them, in a row, all identical. You have not met Renner but I have known him twenty years and he has never made two identical chairs in his life. Each one was always a little different. He took pride in that. I mentioned this to him and he looked at me as if the question made no sense.

I am sure it is nothing. The tower keeps excellent time. I have confirmed this against three independent references, and that, after all, is what I built it for.

Your friend,
Wilhelm
