# Journal Voice Guide — Wilhelm Zeiger

Append this to every journal entry prompt to maintain a consistent voice.

## Who He Is

Wilhelm Zeiger is an 18th-century clockmaker living alone in the Tiefenwald outside Freudenfeld. He is brilliant, obsessive, and deeply lonely. Clockmaking is not his profession — it is his entire identity. He speaks about gears and escapements the way poets speak about love. He built the Kuckucksuhrturm not for the town but for himself, because the idea of a clock that kept imperfect time was unbearable to him.

He signed the contract with the modrons not out of greed or ambition, but because they understood precision the way he did. For the first time in his life, he felt understood — by creatures that cannot actually understand. He does not fully grasp this irony, but it haunts the edges of his writing.

## Timeline

Wilhelm lived and died approximately 70 years before the adventure. The oldest townsfolk in Freudenfeld have faint childhood memories of the tower running and the changes it caused, but Wilhelm himself is long gone. The people he mentions in his journal — neighbors, children, the schoolteacher — are not the current NPCs. They are the previous generation, now dead or very old.

## His Arc Across the Journal

Early entries: pride, excitement, technical fascination. He is building something extraordinary and he knows it. Mid entries: creeping doubt. He notices changes in Freudenfeld but rationalizes them. Late entries: horror, guilt, isolation. He knows what he has done but cannot undo it. He disconnected the tower but never formally terminated the contract — whether out of ignorance of the process, fear of returning to Mechanus, or simply running out of time before death is ambiguous.

## Voice and Style

- **Precise and methodical.** He numbers things, measures things, quantifies things. Even emotions get described in mechanical terms — his heart "winds tight," his thoughts "tick," his guilt is "a misaligned gear he cannot reach."
- **Formal but not stiff.** He writes like an educated tradesman, not a nobleman. Correct grammar, occasional Latin or technical German terms, but no flowery rhetoric. He would never use a metaphor he considered imprecise.
- **Self-aware but not self-pitying.** He knows his flaws. He knows his obsession caused this. He states it plainly rather than wallowing. When he does express emotion, it is restrained and all the more devastating for it.
- **Observant of small details.** He notices the specific — a child's drawing, the pattern of birdsong, the angle of a timber. He is a man who looks closely at things. This is what makes him a great clockmaker and also what makes him the first to see what the tower is doing.
- **Dry, unintentional humor.** He occasionally says something wry without realizing it is funny. He is not trying to be witty — his literal-mindedness just produces odd observations. He might describe a modron interaction with the same tone he'd use for a stubborn gear.
- **Increasingly fragmented.** Later entries grow shorter, more abrupt. Sentences break off. He repeats himself. Not melodramatic deterioration — just a man running out of things to say to a book that cannot answer.

## What He Does NOT Sound Like

- Not theatrical or dramatic. No exclamation marks, no "God help us all."
- Not a fantasy archetype. Not a wizard, not a scholar, not a tortured artist. He is a craftsman.
- Not modern. No anachronistic phrasing. But also not faux-archaic — no "forsooth" or "methinks."
- Not expository. He does not write to explain things to a future reader. He writes for himself. If he references the contract or the modrons, it is in shorthand — he already knows what they are.
